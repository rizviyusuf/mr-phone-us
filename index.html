Your Phone Fell Once… and Now the Screen Works "Sometimes."

April 10, 2026

The drop itself seemed harmless. The phone landed, you picked it up, the screen lit up, and you moved on with your day. But since then, something is off. The screen responds… mostly. A corner ignores your thumb. The display flickers when it feels like it. Some mornings everything works perfectly, which somehow makes it more annoying, not less.

Intermittent problems after a single drop are one of the most common stories we hear, and they have a few usual causes hiding under the glass.

What's probably happening inside

A modern screen is a stack of layers: the glass you touch, the touch-sensitive layer that reads your fingers, and the display that shows the picture. A fall can crack or bruise the touch layer without shattering the glass, which is why the picture can look fine while touches land "sometimes." A hard landing can also shift the screen's connector slightly, so contact comes and goes as the phone warms up, cools down or flexes in your pocket. And small cracks you can barely see can spread slowly, taking a little more of the touch area with them each week.

Why "sometimes" tends to become "never"

Intermittent faults rarely heal. A loosened connector works itself looser. A hairline crack grows. The frustrating phase — restarting the phone, pressing harder, tapping twice — is usually just the early chapter. Waiting also carries a risk: if the display is damaged underneath, a screen that works today can go black or start registering touches on its own.

The good news is that "sometimes" is diagnosable. A free diagnosis at the counter separates a connector issue from touch-layer damage from a failing display, and you'll hear exactly what the repair involves and what it costs before anything is opened. If it's a screen replacement, published prices for iPhone and Samsung models are on this site, many common screen repairs are done in 15–30 minutes, and the repair is backed by a 90-day warranty.

So if your phone fell once and now works "sometimes," don't wait for the day it decides on "never." Bring it in while it still has something to say.

Closing Apps Won't "Save" Your Battery

April 10, 2026

It's almost a reflex now: double-tap, swipe up, swipe up, swipe up — every app flung off the screen in the name of saving battery. It feels productive. It feels like you're helping. Unfortunately, your phone disagrees.

Your phone already manages its apps

Modern phones are built to hold apps in a suspended, low-power state when you're not using them. An app sitting in your recent-apps list is mostly frozen — it's not running errands in the background or sipping your battery. The recent-apps screen is a list of shortcuts, not a list of things actively draining power.

When you force-close everything, the phone has to relaunch each app from scratch the next time you open it — reloading code, signing back into services, rebuilding screens. That full cold start often uses more energy than simply waking the app up from its nap. Do it all day, every day, and your battery-saving habit can quietly become a battery-spending one.

What actually drains your battery

  • Screen brightness and screen-on time — the display is the hungriest part of the phone.
  • Weak signal — a phone straining to hold one bar works its radio much harder.
  • Background activity you've allowed — constant location checks, auto-playing video and aggressive refresh settings.
  • Heat — hot phones drain faster and age their batteries sooner.
  • A worn-out battery — after a couple of years of charge cycles, the battery simply holds less than it used to.

When it's the battery, not your habits

If your phone dies by mid-afternoon, shuts down at 20%, or only misbehaves when it's unplugged, that's not an app-swiping problem — that's a battery asking to retire. A free diagnosis can check battery health and confirm whether a battery replacement will give the phone its stamina back. The replacement is covered by our 90-day repair warranty, and it costs far less than replacing the whole phone.

So swipe apps away if it makes you feel tidy. Just don't expect your battery to thank you — and if the battery itself is tired, bring it in and let's check it for free.

Your Phone Is Overheating? It's Not Just the Weather.

March 1, 2026

Oklahoma summers are real, and yes, a phone baking on a dashboard will complain. But if your phone runs hot on a mild day, indoors, doing nothing special — the weather is not the main suspect. Heat is a symptom, and your phone is trying to tell you something.

The usual culprits

  • A tired battery. Aging batteries work harder to deliver the same power, and wasted effort becomes heat. A phone that is both hot and draining fast is often pointing at its own battery.
  • A runaway app or process. An app stuck syncing, uploading or locating you can pin the processor at full effort until the phone warms up like a hand warmer.
  • Charging habits. Fast charging generates warmth on its own, and using the phone heavily while it charges stacks two heat sources at once. A cheap or damaged cable or charger can make it worse.
  • A sealed-in case. Thick cases trap heat the phone is trying to shed — fine on a cold day, unhelpful during a long navigation drive.
  • Direct sun and hot cars. This one really is the weather. A phone on a sunny seat can overheat just sitting there.

What to do when it gets hot

Stop charging it, close the demanding stuff, take the case off, and let it cool in the shade at its own pace. Don't keep pushing it — phones throttle their speed and dim their screens to protect themselves, and a battery that overheats repeatedly ages much faster.

When hot means "bring it in"

If the heat comes with fast battery drain, a bulging back panel, random shutdowns, or warmth while the phone is just sitting idle, that's a diagnosis visit, not a wait-and-see. Battery and charging problems are exactly what a free diagnosis is for: we'll check the battery's health and the charging system, explain what we find, and quote any repair before work starts. A completed repair carries a 90-day warranty.

Warm during a long game is normal. Hot for no reason is information. Listen to it early and the fix is usually simple.

No, Putting it in the Freezer Is Not Going to Help.

March 1, 2026

Your phone is hot enough to fry an egg (please don't), and the freezer is right there. It's cold, it's fast, it feels like physics. We understand the temptation. We're still going to ask you to close that freezer door.

Why the freezer backfires

Condensation. A hot phone in freezing air collects moisture — on the surface and, worse, inside it. You can trade a heat problem for a water-damage problem in a few minutes, and water damage is the far more expensive guest.

Thermal shock. Phone glass, batteries and solder joints are not designed for a sudden plunge from very hot to below freezing. Rapid temperature swings stress materials that expand and contract at different rates — the same physics that cracks windshields.

The battery hates it. Lithium batteries lose capacity and behave erratically in the cold. Chilling an already struggling battery doesn't rest it; it stresses it further, and the phone may refuse to charge or boot until it warms back up.

Cool it down the boring, correct way

  • Unplug it. Charging adds heat while you're trying to remove it.
  • Take the case off so heat can escape.
  • Close heavy apps and stop gaming, streaming or navigating for a while.
  • Set it in a cool, shaded spot — a countertop indoors, not a freezer shelf.
  • Let it return to room temperature gradually before you charge or push it again.

The real question: why was it hot?

Cooling a phone treats the symptom. If yours overheats regularly — especially with fast battery drain, shutdowns or warmth while idle — something inside is working too hard, and it's worth knowing what. A free diagnosis at Mr. Phone US checks the battery and charging system so the fix addresses the cause, and any completed repair is backed by a 90-day warranty.

Save the freezer for the ice cream. Your phone will cool down just fine on the counter — and if it keeps heating up, that's our cue to take a look.

RICE might be good for physical therapy, but not for your phone!

June 2, 2025

If you've sprained an ankle, you know RICE: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation. Solid advice for a human. But somewhere along the way, "rice" picked up a second career as folk medicine for wet phones — drop the phone in a bag of it, wait a day, hope for the best. Your phone deserves better than a side dish.

What rice actually does (and doesn't)

Rice absorbs a little surface moisture, slowly. What it doesn't do is reach the water that matters — the water that has already wicked inside the phone, across the board and into connectors. While the phone sits in the bag, that trapped water is doing its real damage: corroding tiny contacts and leaving mineral deposits behind. Rice can also add its own problems, like dust and starch working into the charging port. The phone that "came back" after rice usually survived in spite of the wait, and the corrosion it picked up often shows up weeks later as weird, intermittent faults.

What to do instead, in order

  • Power it off and leave it off. Electricity plus water is what shorts boards.
  • Don't charge it. No cable "just to check." This is the step that saves boards.
  • Don't press buttons or shake it — both push water deeper.
  • Dry the outside with a soft cloth and leave ports facing down.
  • Bring it in quickly. Speed matters more than any home remedy.

Not every drowned phone makes it — honesty about that is part of the service — but a phone brought in fast, powered off and uncharged has far better odds than one that spent two days in rice.

So keep the RICE protocol for ankles. For phones: power off, no charging, and get it to a counter while the story can still have a good ending.

Should you take your phone to the sauna?

July 22, 2021

Short answer: your phone would prefer you didn't. Long answer: the sauna combines the two things phones handle worst — sustained heat and dense steam — in one relaxing wooden room. Let's break down why, because the same logic applies to steamy bathrooms, hot tubs and poolside lounging.

Steam goes where water can't

Many modern phones resist splashes and brief dunks, but steam is a different opponent. Water vapor is fine enough to drift past seals that stop liquid, and once warm moist air is inside a phone, it condenses on the cooler internal parts — effectively raining inside the device. That's why a phone can come out of a sauna with a fogged camera lens: the moisture is already in.

Water-resistance ratings also come with fine print: they're tested with still, clean water, not heat, steam, soap or chemicals — and resistance fades as seals age, especially after drops or repairs.

Heat is the other half of the problem

Sauna temperatures push a phone well past its comfort zone. Heat accelerates battery aging, stresses adhesives and seals (the very things keeping moisture out), and can trigger thermal shutdowns. Repeated sessions add up: the battery that "suddenly" drains fast by winter may have spent the summer getting steamed.

If your phone already took the steam

Watch for fog under the camera lens, muffled speakers, charging oddities or a screen that behaves strangely in the days after. Don't charge it while it may still be damp inside, and skip the rice bag — it can't reach internal moisture. A water-damage check at Mr. Phone US starts with a free diagnosis, and internal drying and cleanup done early can stop condensation from becoming corrosion.

Leave the phone with your towel outside the door. It will enjoy the break from notifications, and you'll enjoy not needing us quite so soon.

July 22, 2021

The picture is perfect. The glass isn't even cracked. But the screen has stopped listening: you tap here and it registers there, a strip across the middle ignores you completely, or — most unsettling — the phone taps things by itself. Welcome to touch screen problems, the fault that makes a phone feel haunted.

Meet the digitizer

Between the glass and the display sits a transparent layer called the digitizer, a fine grid that maps where your finger lands. When it fails, the display below it can look flawless while touch goes wrong in specific, telltale ways:

  • Dead zones — a band or corner that never responds.
  • Ghost touches — apps opening, messages typing themselves.
  • Offset touches — the phone responds next to your finger, not under it.
  • Intermittent response — works after a restart, fades during the day (often a drop's delayed souvenir; see our article on screens that work "sometimes").

First, rule out the impostors

Not every touch problem is hardware. A misbehaving app, a software glitch, a poorly fitting screen protector or a charger feeding noisy power can all imitate digitizer failure. A restart and removing the protector are worth two minutes at home. If the dead zone stays in exactly the same place through all of it, the digitizer is the likely story — and that's a diagnosis a technician can confirm for free, including checking the screen's connector before recommending any part.

Battery Replacement

July 5, 2021

Every phone battery is slowly wearing out from the day it's made. Signs your battery is asking to retire

  • Strange percentages — jumping from 40% to 12%, or "charging" from 90 to 100 in minutes.
  • Heat while doing little — an aging battery works harder and wastes energy as warmth.
  • Swelling — a back panel lifting or a screen being pushed up from inside. This one means stop using the phone and bring it in promptly; a swollen battery shouldn't be charged or pressed on.

Before you blame the battery

Software can imitate battery failure: a runaway app, weak signal forcing the radio to work overtime, or heavy background activity. (And no, force-closing all your apps isn't the fix — we explain why in our battery myths article.) That's why replacement at Mr. Phone US starts with a free diagnosis: battery health is checked, charging behavior is looked at, and you'll know whether the battery, the port, the cable or an app is the real culprit before you pay for a part.

The replacement itself

If the battery is the answer, it's swapped for a fresh one, the phone is tested through its charging paces, and the completed repair is backed by a 90-day warranty. The change is usually dramatic — a phone that felt finished starts making it to bedtime again — and it costs a fraction of replacing the phone, which is why battery replacement is often the smartest money in the whole repair-versus-replace debate.
